Bitcoin price alerts

Name a BTC price, pick a direction, and get an email or Telegram message the moment the market crosses it. Free, and there is no password to create.

Bitcoin is the pair people most often want an alert on and the one where waiting at a chart is least practical, because the level you care about is usually hours or days away. TriggerX watches BTCUSDT on the live Binance feed and notifies you when your condition is met, once.

Three ways to describe the level

  • Above. Fires when BTC trades over your number. For watching a breakout level.
  • Below. Fires when BTC trades under it. For watching a support level you care about.
  • Crosses. Fires from either direction, so one alert covers both sides of a level instead of two.

Or just say the move

If you do not have an exact number in mind, describe the move instead: BTC up 10%. TriggerX reads the live price when you create the alert, computes the target from it, and arms the alert at that figure. The percentage is resolved once, at creation time. It is a shortcut for arithmetic, not a trailing alert that keeps re-basing as the price moves.

Plain-English input works for absolute levels too, so BTC above 90000 and BTC below 70000 are both parsed without touching a form.

How delivery works

  1. Sign in with email. A one-time code, no password to invent or store.
  2. Arm the alert. Pick BTCUSDT and a condition, or type it in plain English.
  3. Close the tab. Monitoring is server-side, so nothing needs to stay open.
  4. Get notified once. Email or Telegram fires on the crossing, then the alert deactivates and moves to your history.

Why one notification

A price that hovers around a level will cross it repeatedly, and an alerting tool that fires on every crossing becomes noise you learn to ignore. TriggerX deactivates an alert after it fires, so you get exactly one message per condition. If you want to keep watching the same level, arm it again.

TriggerX is built to inform, not to replace your trading safeguards. No alert service can guarantee delivery every time. Network interruptions, exchange feed delays and downtime can all make a notification late or missed. For risk that actually matters, use exchange-native stop-losses or limit orders, which execute without depending on you reading a message.

Questions

How do I set a Bitcoin price alert?

Sign in with an emailed one-time code, pick BTCUSDT, and name a price with a direction: above, below, or crosses. You can also type it in plain English, for example BTC above 90000, and the target is parsed for you.

Can I get an alert for a percentage move in BTC instead of a price?

Yes. Describe the move rather than the level, for example BTC up 10%. TriggerX reads the live price at the moment you create the alert, works out the resulting target, and arms the alert at that number.

How fast does a Bitcoin alert arrive?

Prices stream from Binance over a WebSocket rather than being polled, so a crossing is detected within the same second. Telegram delivery is close to instant; email typically lands within a few seconds.

Do I need to keep a browser tab open?

No. Monitoring runs server-side, so you can close everything after arming the alert and still be notified.
